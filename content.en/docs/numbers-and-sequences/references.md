"To me the numbers that are beautiful are not beautiful at all if they are not meaningful" — Paul Erdos

Srinivasa Ramanujan was a great Indian mathematical genius born into a poor family in Erode. Even as a child he showed exceptional mathematical talent and the ability to perform calculations at lightning speed. He derived thousands of formulae and recorded them in his three notebooks, which are still preserved at the University of Madras today. With the help of several benefactors, he became the first research scholar of the University of Madras. He later went to England and, from 1914 to 1919, carried out research together with the mathematician G.H. Hardy.

Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920)

Ramanujan was deeply interested in investigating the structure of numbers, and as a result he created countless new ideas in analytic number theory. His mathematical ability is compared to that of great mathematicians such as Euler and Jacobi. Ramanujan produced about 30 research papers on his own and 7 more jointly with G.H. Hardy. In his short lifespan of 32 years, he created 3972 formulae and theorems. In recognition of his research, Cambridge University awarded him the B.A. research degree in 1916, equivalent to today's Ph.D. degree. For his contributions to number theory, he was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society (F.R.S.) in 1918.

Ramanujan's discoveries continue to fascinate mathematicians across the world even today. The notes he composed towards the end of his life, nearly a century ago, remain connected to modern science.
