## If $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$, then the value of $2\tan^2\theta + \sin^2\theta - 1$ is :

---

#### Since $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$, dividing both sides by $\cos\theta$ gives $\tan\theta = 1$, which implies $\theta = 45^\circ$.

For $\theta = 45^\circ$:
- $\tan 45^\circ = 1 \implies \tan^2 45^\circ = 1$
- $\sin 45^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \sin^2 45^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$

Substituting into the expression:

<div class="math-display">$$2\tan^2\theta + \sin^2\theta - 1 = 2(1)^2 + \frac{1}{2} - 1 = 2 - 1 + \frac{1}{2} = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$$</div>
.
