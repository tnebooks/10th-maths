## If the roots of the equation $q^2x^2+p^2x+r^2=0$ are the squares of the roots of the equation $qx^2+px+r=0$, then $q,p,r$ are in _______

---

#### Let the roots of $qx^2+px+r=0$ be $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then $\alpha+\beta = -\frac{p}{q}$ and $\alpha\beta = \frac{r}{q}$. The given second equation has roots $\alpha^2$ and $\beta^2$, so the sum of its roots is $\alpha^2+\beta^2 = -\frac{p^2}{q^2}$. We also know $\alpha^2+\beta^2 = (\alpha+\beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta = \frac{p^2}{q^2} - \frac{2r}{q}$. Equating the two expressions: $-\frac{p^2}{q^2} = \frac{p^2}{q^2} - \frac{2r}{q} \implies \frac{2p^2}{q^2} = \frac{2r}{q} \implies p^2 = qr$. Since $p^2 = qr$, $q, p, r$ are in Geometric Progression (G.P.).
