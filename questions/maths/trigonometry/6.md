## If $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$, then $2\tan^2\theta + \sin^2\theta - 1$ is equal to

---

#### Since $\sin\theta = \cos\theta$, dividing by $\cos\theta$ gives $\tan\theta = 1$, which implies $\theta = 45^\circ$.
For $\theta = 45^\circ$:
$$\tan 45^\circ = 1 \implies \tan^2 45^\circ = 1$$
$$\sin 45^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \sin^2 45^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$$
Substituting these values into the expression:
$$2\tan^2\theta + \sin^2\theta - 1 = 2(1) + \frac{1}{2} - 1 = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$$
